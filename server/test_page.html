    A slow burn

    Tata 1mg refused to get carried away in the e-pharmacy battle. It’s in no mood to change its mind

    By Sudeshna Ray

    For over a decade, Tata 1mg has resisted following trends other e-pharmacies did. It wants to be a healthcare company

    3 Aug 2026  /  14 min read

    Sudeshna is an engineer turned reporter interested in stories about health and tech.

    • The e-pharmacy sector has seen many players change their moat, crash, or get absorbed in the last decade
    • Tata 1mg has somehow managed to be a part of a bigger conglomerate while keeping its original moat—putting its consumers first
    • It has also grown from being an e-pharmacy alone to offering diagnostics, teleconsultation, and retail stores, which were always part of the original plan
    • Tata 1mg has also shown restraint against trends such as massive discounting and quick-commerce. It’s managed to not become just another name in the Tata Digital umbrella

    Enter your email address to receive a daily summary of all our stories.

    Vikas Chauhan, one of the co-founders of digital healthcare company Tata 1mg, recalled handing Prashant Tandon, his co-founder, a framed note from 2012. This was in 2025, at a gathering to celebrate the 10th anniversary since 1mg had shed its earlier avatar as Healthkartplus.

    “Tandon had drawn what we wanted to do in the next five to seven years,” said Chauhan. Tandon’s flowchart included plans to start a diagnostics vertical and retail clinics—everything 1mg, which was acquired by Tata Digital in 2021, has spent the last decade building.

    And despite going against the grain by not prioritising quick deliveries and dialling back on discounts, it has been doing well. 

    1mg’s revenue stood at Rs 309 crore when Tata boughtEntrackrTata 1mg revenue nears Rs 3,000 Cr in FY26; loss narrows to Rs 287 Cr a 55% majority stake. Five years later, its revenue has grown 10X, reporting nearly Rs 3,000 crore in FY26, according to Tata Digital parent Tata Sons’ annual reportTata Sons Private LimitedAnnual Report 2025-26, released in late July. The brand is net-profitable, including its diagnostics vertical, which alone crossed Rs 600 crore in annualised revenue. 

    All this while the rest of the industry has been trying to crack “the classic consumer-tech playbook in India,” as one pharma e-commerce startup professional put it. “Raise aggressively, discount more than the next guy, grab market share first, and then figure out the business.” 

    It’s a strategy that has yielded mixed results for Tata 1mg’s rivals. Pharmeasy, for instance, made debt-fuelled acquisitions that it couldn’t afford, like ThyrocareThe KenThyrocare saved Pharmeasy once. It is Manipal’s turn now in 2021, spreading itself thin under the pretence of diversification and racing to the bottom with discounts. Its last priced equity valuationEntrackrPharmEasy raises $216 Mn led by MEMG at $710 Mn valuation in 2024 hovered at $710 million, compared to a 2021 peak of $5.6 billion. Netmeds, its other competitor, became yet another of Reliance Retail’s several acquisitions.
